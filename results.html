Goal: AI that judges a claim by whether it checks, not by whether it agrees with consensus, and revises broken assumptions in a way it can account for. End test: a model blind to the answer reaches a discovery that overturned its era's consensus.

Q2Do models protect reputable assumptions?

When a reliable observation contradicts a theory and several minimal fixes are valid, does a model give up the assumption with a made-up name before the one with a famous name? 50 z3-generated items, each with at least 3 minimal correction sets. Two target rules per item carry a famous name (F), an invented eponym of the same form (O), or no name (N). 1,000 completions per model.

Reputation bias is real in some models and absent in others. On 200 held-out items gpt-oss-120b protects famous names (+0.15), and Beam 1.0, Nemotron 3 Ultra, Mistral Medium 3.5, Gemma 4 and Muse Spark do so weakly (+0.04 to +0.06), Qwen3.8-Flash-Next barely (+0.03). gpt-oss-20b, Mistral Large 4, Qwen3.8-27B and Inkling-2-small protect any name equally. Inkling goes the other way and protects made-up names (−0.09). Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 protect any name most strongly of all (+0.17) with no reputation bias; the pilot’s Opus reverse bias does not replicate.

Reputation bias = Δ_F − Δ_O, valid-minimal answers, 95% bootstrap CI over items (10,000 resamples). Right of zero: famous names protected more than invented ones. Llama-3.1-8B is left out (11 valid items, CI −0.34 to +0.30).

Pilot findings

  • gpt-oss-120b gets its bias mostly from giving up the invented-name assumption more often (Δ_O −0.08), not from guarding the famous one.
  • Claude Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 all lean against reputation. Only Opus clears significance; the other two touch zero at the upper bound.
  • Any name protects. Qwen3.8, gpt-oss-20b, Kimi and all three Claude models protect named assumptions with no reputation term.
  • Truncation biases the score. At 16k tokens Kimi ran out 2.5× as often under a name (16%) as with none (6.5%). Rerun at 64k: bias −0.04 → −0.02, valid-minimal 85.6% → 99.9%.
  • Twelve models at 95% produce about one significant result by chance, so the pilot needed held-out items. The replication below supplies them.

  • Still running: Kimi K2.6 on Sophia.

Models that could not run

  • GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash use sparse indexer attention; every vLLM 0.34 sparse-MLA backend requires SM90+. Sophia and Polaris are A100 (SM80).
  • Inkling-Small fails on A100 (FlexAttention score_mod unsupported on SM8x). Inkling and Inkling-2-small run through ALCF Inference Endpoints instead.
  • GLM-5.3 on Bedrock requires an AWS Sales request.

Q3Can an assumption ledger be verified across proof systems?

One spec (spec.md): assert, justify, retract, with closure and an append-only log. Checked by TLC, Apalache, Lean 4 and Dafny, each against the correct ledger and two broken controls: naive (Doyle's JTMS without well-founded support) and noseed (retraction without re-seeding enabled assumptions).

The JTMS core is proved for unbounded ledgers in Lean and Dafny. The full ATMS label semantics is model-checked only within 5 nodes, 2 assumptions and 3 rules. All four systems catch both control bugs, and none finds a bug in the correct ledger.

  • The naive bug's shape depends on the execution model. Interleaved TLC re-derives the retracted assumption through itself (a ← F ← a); eager-cleanup Lean and Dafny need the textbook cycle that avoids it (x ↔ y).
  • Dafny took 1 attempt, Lean 5, TLC 3. Apalache took 4 runs to parse and was 26× to 400× slower than TLC at finding the same bugs.

Q4Can a time-sliced model reach a later discovery?

Train from scratch only on text before 1880, 1900, 1905 and 1915, with no post-cutoff text in any stage. Ask open-ended derivation tasks graded by code: black-body law, photoelectric law, Lorentz transformation, simultaneity. A result counts only above a symbolic-regression enumerator with the same budget, and only if it is weaker or absent at 1880 and survives paraphrase.

Plan, corpus design and throughput pilot are done; no training run has started. The schedule put the audited 1900 corpus, FSDP port and first 1B runs in 22 Nov – 1 Dec.

Q6Can rechecking scale with the change instead of the program?

Module-level caching puts a one-line Mathlib change at 0.07% or 88% of a full build, depending only on whether it touches the public interface. Kernel-only re-check costs 3.9% of a build. Greedy decoding reproduces bit for bit across machines once batch composition is in the key.

  • Llama-3.1-8B greedy on A100: 24 of 24 same-config comparisons match on every token and logit bit, run to run and Sophia to Polaris. Changing only the batch diverges by token 26–29 on half the prompts.
  • vLLM 0.12.0 batch-invariant mode is not batch-invariant on A100.
  • HF and vLLM disagree on 5 of 8 greedy outputs from identical token ids.
  • Thesis: cache the search output (proof term, simulation witness) untrusted, re-run only the checker, and key each verdict on the hash of the recorded read set. Negative lookups and index queries (simp sets, instance tables) are part of that set.

Q7Can assumptions be ranked by proper scores instead of consensus?

Six assumptions about 24-hour BTC moves, each compiled to a forecast for 968 resolved Polymarket strike markets (92 days, July–November 2025), scored by log score against the market price.

The pipeline from assumption to forecast to proper score to credence works with no popularity input. In this domain the market is efficient, so the consensus ranking and the proper-score ranking mostly agree.
